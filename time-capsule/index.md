  <header class="capsule-part-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Part 1</p>
    <h2 id="part-1-title">The Objects</h2>
  </header>

<div class="capsule-opening">
  <p>I’ve never thought of myself as a nostalgic person.</p>
  <p>I’ve never made a conscious effort to hold on to things, because I feel like I’m always moving.</p>
  <p>People, places, and things in my life keep changing, and I’ve never felt much need to hold on to something just to remember them by.</p>
  <p>But there is one strange exception.</p>
</div>

  <header class="capsule-section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Objects 1–2</p>
    <h2 id="blankies-title">My Two Blankies</h2>
  </header>

      <p>I’m lucky to still have it with me today. I sleep holding it almost every night. I’ve probably spent fewer than forty nights without it in my entire life.</p>

  <div class="capsule-replacement-overview">

    <div class="capsule-copy">
      <p>When I was three or four, my mom bought an identical blanket as a replacement. But I never felt the same way about it.</p>
      <p>Looking at it now, it’s much closer to what my original blanky looked like when I was little. My original has changed so much over the years, while this one still looks familiar. Even so, it has never felt like my blanky.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="capsule-touch">
    <h3>The places I reach for</h3>
    <div class="capsule-touch-layout">
    <div class="capsule-touch-images">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/compare.png" alt="Detail comparison of the original and replacement blankets" loading="lazy">
      </figure>
    </div>
    <div class="capsule-copy">
      <p>There are two places on my blanky that I especially like to touch. Whenever it’s beside me, I often find myself reaching for it and rubbing those spots without even thinking.</p>
      <p>It feels like the habit is written into my code. Sometimes I wonder whether it distracts me, but the movement is so familiar that I barely notice I’m doing it.</p>
      <p>When I first started rubbing these spots, my blanky probably still looked and felt much like the replacement shown below. After years of wear, the texture must have changed a lot. But the worn-out fabric is what feels familiar to me now. The replacement might be closer to how my blanky originally felt, yet it doesn’t give me the same feeling.</p>
      <p>I wonder if I’ve been getting used to each small change without noticing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
    </div>

  <div class="capsule-closing capsule-copy">
    <p>My blanky keeps wearing out, and I know it won’t last forever. That thought has been with me for a long time. I can see it in the thinning fabric and fraying edges, but I still hold it every night and reach for it during the day.</p>
    <p>This project has made me pay closer attention to something that has always felt like a given. Taking these photos and writing about it is a way of recording the details I usually take for granted: how it looks now, how I touch it, and how much of my everyday life includes it.</p>
    <p>I don’t know whether documenting it will make losing it any easier. But maybe it’s a way to begin preparing for that, while it’s still here.</p>
  </div>

</section>

<section class="capsule-part capsule-part--pending" id="part-2" aria-labelledby="part-2-title">
  <header class="capsule-part-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Part 2 / In progress</p>
    <h2 id="part-2-title">Design the Time Capsule</h2>
  </header>
  <div class="capsule-copy">
    <p>I’ve often trusted intuition in design. In past projects, an idea would sometimes arrive suddenly, and I would know how I wanted to move forward. That hasn’t happened with this capsule yet. None of the ideas feels quite right. Maybe it’s because my blanky matters so much to me.</p>
    <p>Lately, I’ve realized that my attachment to my blanky is also what’s wearing it out. It bothers me to see it getting thinner, especially knowing I can’t replace it. But I still use it every day, and I don’t want to stop.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="capsule-prototype-step">
    <h3>Choosing the inner container</h3>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/empty-storage-box.jpeg" alt="The empty lidless storage box I took with me to look for an outer layer" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>The lidless storage box I started with.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>I decided to start with something practical: a rigid inner container to support my blanky. I had a lidless storage box that was about the right size. I’m worried that taking the blanky directly out of an outer bag, without that support, could pull on the worn fabric and tear it. With the box, I can lift the whole container out instead.</p>
        <p>I also chose it for its smooth surface. A fuzzy lining might seem softer, but I’m concerned that its fibers could catch in the blanky and cause more damage. A smooth container feels like a safer choice to me.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="capsule-prototype-step">
    <h3>Seeing the difference in thickness</h3>
    <div class="capsule-photo-pair capsule-prototype-comparison">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/replacement-in-box.jpeg" alt="The thicker replacement blanket folded in the storage box" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>The replacement blanket in the box.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/original-in-box.jpeg" alt="My thinner original blanky folded in the same storage box, leaving more space above it" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>My original blanky in the same box.</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </div>
    <div class="capsule-copy capsule-prototype-reflection">
      <p>While trying the box, I folded each blanket and put it inside in turn. I could already feel the difference in thickness by touch, but seeing them in the same container made it much more obvious how thin my original blanky has become over the years.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="capsule-prototype-step">
    <h3>Trying the outer layer</h3>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/capsule-packaging.jpeg" alt="My original blanky in a storage box fitted inside a clear zippered bedsheet package" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>Trying the storage box inside the bedsheet packaging.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>I took the box with me to look for an outer layer. At a general store, I found a set of bedsheets whose packaging looked like a good fit for the box, so I bought it.</p>
        <p>The clear zippered package fits around the box and lets me see the blanky inside. This is my first try at putting the two together.</p>
        <p>Functionally, this meets my needs: the smooth, rigid inner container supports the blanky, and the transparent outer layer is easy to open and close.</p>
        <p>My next step is to work on the interaction design: how someone opens the capsule and encounters my blanky. I want that experience to convey the tension between my attachment to it and the wear that comes with using it. I want the design to acknowledge that feeling without making the choice seem more dramatic than it is.</p>
        <p>I’ve considered a few approaches, but some feel too deliberate or too dramatic for what I actually feel. The feeling is real; I just don’t want to force it into a poetic gesture. I’m still figuring out how much the design needs to say.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="capsule-prototype-step">
    <h3>Marking its height</h3>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/dated-height-mark.jpeg" alt="A handwritten date, 2026.10.28, and a height mark on the side of the container" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>A height reference dated October 28, 2026.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>I marked the height of my folded blanky on the side of the container and added the date. This gives me a reference for noticing how it changes over time.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="capsule-prototype-step">
    <h3>Wrapping back the missing thickness</h3>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/1.jpeg" alt="My folded original blanky laid out with several pieces of fabric for wrapping" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>My original blanky and the fabric layers for wrapping it.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>Seeing both blankets in the same container gave me an idea. I could wrap my original blanky in layers of fabric, adding back some of the thickness it has lost over the years.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/2.jpeg" alt="My blanky wrapped in fabric, with a pale green outer layer" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>The blanky wrapped in additional layers.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>I wrapped the blanky until the bundle was thick enough to sit at roughly the same height in the container as the replacement blanket. The added layers stand in for the material that has gradually worn away.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="capsule-row">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/3.jpeg" alt="The wrapped blanky filling the inner container" loading="lazy">
        <figcaption>Trying the wrapped blanky in the same container.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <div class="capsule-copy">
        <p>Placed in the container, the wrapped bundle gives someone a fuller starting point. As they unwrap it layer by layer, it becomes thinner until they reach my blanky as it is today. I want that gradual change to give a sense of how it has worn down through years of use.</p>
        <p>The wrapping is an approximation of the missing thickness. Removing it makes that loss something someone can experience through their hands.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <p class="eyebrow">01 / Setting</p>
      <h3 id="future-title">Establish the Future</h3>
      <p>New York City, around 2151 — 125 years in the future.</p>
      <p>Scanning, reproducing, and restoring physical objects has become routine. Worn or damaged belongings are usually restored, reproduced, or recycled. Few objects remain visibly old, and objects rarely age alongside their owners.</p>
      <p>During the renovation of an old residential building, workers discover a sealed time capsule from the 2020s.</p>
    </section>

    <section class="capsule-narrative-step" aria-labelledby="perspective-title">
      <p class="eyebrow">02 / Perspective</p>
      <h3 id="perspective-title">Choose a Perspective</h3>
      <p>The story follows a child around 10–12 years old who encounters the capsule.</p>
      <p>He recognizes the blanket inside, but cannot understand why someone carefully preserved something so ordinary and worn. Having grown up with effortless restoration, he sees deterioration as a problem with an obvious solution.</p>

    <section class="capsule-narrative-step" aria-labelledby="reflection-title">
      <p class="eyebrow">06 / Reflection</p>
      <h3 id="reflection-title">Reflection</h3>
      <p>I spent a lot of this project trying to find a way to express my attachment to my blanky without making it feel more dramatic than it is. The most useful starting point was putting both blankets into the same container. I already knew mine felt thinner, but seeing the difference gave me something concrete to work with.</p>
      <p>Wrapping it in layers grew out of that comparison. The added fabric roughly fills the space that has been lost through wear. Removing those layers lets another person notice the change in thickness through their own actions. It does not recreate the blanket’s past exactly, but it gives them a way to encounter its present condition.</p>
      <p>Imagining the little boy’s response helped me look at the same object from outside my own attachment. He does not have my memories, so replacing it initially makes sense to him. I wanted his understanding to shift only a little: he still does not know its whole story, but he starts to notice that the worn areas tell him something about how it was used.</p>
      <p>Making the illustrations also reminded me how much specific details matter. Some of the generated damage looked unlike my actual blanket, so I used photographs to guide the revisions. Keeping the worn corner, the container, and the wrapping consistent mattered because these are the things the story asks the viewer to look at closely.</p>
      <p>I still want to use my blanky, even though using it wears it out. This project has not resolved that feeling. What it has given me is a way to share a little of it through the packaging, the unwrapping, and the boy’s decision to put it back.</p>
    </section>
