<div class="capsule-opening">
  <p>I’ve never thought of myself as a nostalgic person.</p>
  <p>I missed both my high school and college graduation ceremonies because of circumstances at the time, but I’ve mostly made peace with that. Things my friends gave me tend to disappear unless I actually use them. When I travel, I rarely keep souvenirs.</p>
  <p>People, places, and things in my life keep changing, and I’ve never felt much need to hold on to something just to remember them by.</p>
  <p>But there is one strange exception.</p>
</div>

    <div class="capsule-copy">
      <p>When I was three or four, my mom bought an identical blanket as a replacement. But I never felt the same way about it.</p>
      <p>Looking at it now, it’s much closer to what my original blanky looked like when I was little. My original has changed so much over the years, while this one still looks familiar. Even so, it has never felt like my blanky.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="capsule-touch">
    <h3>The places I reach for</h3>
    <div class="capsule-touch-layout">
    <div class="capsule-touch-images">
      <figure>
        <img class="capsule-photo" src="assets/images/compare.png" alt="Detail comparison of the original and replacement blankets" loading="lazy">
      </figure>
    </div>
    <div class="capsule-copy">
      <p>There are two places on my blanky that I especially like to touch. Whenever it’s beside me, I often find myself reaching for it and rubbing those spots without even thinking.</p>
      <p>It feels like the habit is written into my code. Sometimes I wonder whether it distracts me, but the movement is so familiar that I barely notice I’m doing it.</p>
      <p>When I first started rubbing these spots, my blanky probably still looked and felt much like the replacement shown below. After years of wear, the texture must have changed a lot. But the worn-out fabric is what feels familiar to me now. The replacement might be closer to how my blanky originally felt, yet it doesn’t give me the same feeling.</p>
      <p>I wonder if I’ve been getting used to each small change without noticing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
    </div>

  <div class="capsule-closing capsule-copy">
    <p>My blanky keeps wearing out, and I know it won’t last forever. That thought has been with me for a long time. I can see it in the thinning fabric and fraying edges, but I still hold it every night and reach for it during the day.</p>
    <p>This project has made me pay closer attention to something that has always felt like a given. Taking these photos and writing about it is a way of recording the details I usually take for granted: how it looks now, how I touch it, and how much of my everyday life includes it.</p>
    <p>I don’t know whether documenting it will make losing it any easier. But maybe it’s a way to begin preparing for that, while it’s still here.</p>
  </div>
